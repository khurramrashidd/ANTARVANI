अन्तर्वाणी — the voice within

Twenty Hindi care phrases, read from the movement of the mouth alone.

People who cannot speak — after a laryngectomy, with a tracheostomy, during intensive care — are often left pointing at a card. ANTARVANI reads the phrase off their lips with an ordinary Android phone. No microphone, no electrodes, nothing worn on the face.

What the model actually receives — 88×88 greyscale, 16 fps

Losing speech does not mean losing language.

The words are still there. What is missing is a way out of the body that other people can hear — and the tools meant to help are mostly built for English, for a keyboard, or for a sensor nobody wants taped to their throat.

Boards are slow

Pointing at a laminated card gets a thirsty patient a glass of water in the time it takes to find the picture. Most boards carry a dozen symbols and nothing a person would actually say.

Sensors are intrusive

Surface electrodes, throat microphones and ultrasound probes all work, and all require equipment on the face or neck of someone already having a hard day.

Hindi is underserved

Published lip-reading corpora are overwhelmingly English. A hospital ward in Maharashtra needs the phrases people there say, in the language they say them in.

Twenty phrases that matter at a bedside.

Chosen for need, not for how easy they are to tell apart. Each is a whole utterance, recorded in a fixed three-second window, so the model learns a phrase rather than stitching one together from letters.

Camera to phrase, entirely on the phone.

Nothing is uploaded at the moment of use, and no audio is captured at any stage of the project — not during collection, not during inference.

Record

Three seconds from the front camera. The microphone is never opened.

Find the face

MediaPipe returns 468 landmarks per frame, on device.

Align and crop

The box is rotated to level the eyes and scaled by the distance between them, so the mouth fills the same area whatever the camera distance.

Recognise

48 frames of 88×88 greyscale through a 3D CNN, 3.56 MB as TFLite.

Personalise

The result is blended with prototypes built from a handful of the user's own recordings.

Speak

The phrase is read aloud in Hindi by the phone.

Recorded by us, from scratch.

No dataset existed for this, so we built one — with a purpose-made Android app that handles consent, speaker numbering, quality checks and upload, so every clip arrives named, attributed and verifiable.

Small enough to live on the phone.

Five strided 3D convolutions reduce the clip in space and time, two 1D convolutions read across what is left, and a global average gives a 192-dimensional embedding. No MaxPool3D and no recurrent layer: TFLite supports neither, and a model that cannot be converted cannot ship.

Few-shot personalisation

A model that has never met you is guessing at a face it has not seen. Ours builds a prototype per phrase from a few of the user's own clips and blends it with the general model's embedding. This is the part of the project we are putting forward as the contribution, and it is what makes a 20-phrase vocabulary workable for one real person rather than for an average of forty.

Updating without a new install

Retrained models are published from the training notebook and picked up over the air: the app verifies a SHA-256 checksum, swaps the model in and carries on. No store release, no reinstall, and no clip waiting on a phone is ever lost to an update.

Where it stands.

Every number on this page comes out of the training run itself. Nothing here is typed in by hand.

Rules we set before the first recording.

No audio, ever

The microphone is not opened during collection or during use. A silent speech interface that records sound would be collecting exactly the thing its users cannot produce, and exactly the thing a ward has no right to capture.

Consent, per person

Every volunteer signs a numbered consent form before recording, and separately decides whether their clips may ever be published. Nobody under eighteen is recorded. A volunteer can ask for their recordings to be deleted from inside the app, and the deletion is logged.

Names are not data

Volunteers appear here and in every export as a speaker number. Names live in a private roster the team signs in to see. They are never in the dataset manifest and never on this page.

The phone, not the cloud

Recognition runs on the device. Clips are uploaded only during corpus collection, to a folder the project controls, and never at the moment somebody is using the app to say they are in pain.
